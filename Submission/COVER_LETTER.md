# Cover Letter — Clinical Nutrition

**Date:** July 24, 2026

**To:** The Editor-in-Chief, Clinical Nutrition

**Re:** Submission of Original Research Article

Dear Editor,

We submit our manuscript, **"Composite Nutritional Indices Predict Mortality Across Cancer Types: A Pooled NHANES Analysis With In-Depth Gastrointestinal Validation"** for consideration as a Full Length Article in *Clinical Nutrition*.

Nutritional assessment is a cornerstone of cancer care, yet the evidence for PNI, CONUT, and GNRI rests on single-cancer cohorts without cross-cancer comparison or population-level GI validation with time-dependent and competing-risk decomposition. Our study addresses these gaps using two NHANES generations spanning 30 years (NHANES III 1988–1994 + Continuous 2005–2016).

We acknowledge two important limitations of NHANES for this question and have calibrated our conclusions accordingly throughout the manuscript: (1) NHANES is a **prevalent cohort** — participants are sampled after cancer diagnosis, introducing length-biased sampling and precluding adjustment for time since diagnosis; (2) **cancer stage and treatment data are unavailable**, and the PNI E-value (1.66) is insufficient to exclude confounding by disease severity. These constraints are inherent to NHANES and are explicitly discussed in the manuscript.

Within these constraints, the study offers the following contributions:

1. **Cross-cancer comparison with cancer-type interaction.** Across 2,942 patients (five cancer types), CONUT showed significant effect modification by cancer type (interaction p=0.016), and index performance paralleled the underlying malnutrition burden of each malignancy. NHANES III (1988–1994) extends temporal breadth beyond the overlapping Li et al. 2025 study.

2. **In-depth GI mechanistic analysis.** In 353 GI cancer patients with 189 deaths, survey-weighted models identified CONUT as the only index robust to design-based confounding. PNI decomposed to albumin alone — lymphocyte count contributed nothing. Time-dependent and competing-risk analyses showed PNI operates through non-cancer mortality and is concentrated in the first 2 years.

3. **Transparent limitation reporting.** We report E-values (1.66–1.97), survey-weighted sensitivity (GI PNI HR 0.87, p=0.432), and the competing-risk patterns. The data cannot distinguish between a true nutritional effect and confounding by disease severity — a distinction we maintain throughout.

We believe the methodological rigor (time-dependent Cox, competing risks, decomposition, E-values, survey-weighting) and the candid limitation reporting make this work suitable for *Clinical Nutrition*. Analysis code is available at https://github.com/woodhaha/nhanes-nutritional-indices.

This manuscript has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission and have no conflicts of interest.
