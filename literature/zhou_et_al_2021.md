# Association Between the Prognostic Nutritional Index and Cognitive Function Among Older Adults in the United States: A Population-Based Study

- **Authors**: Jie Zhou, Luping Ma, Lulei Zhao, Jiamin Sheng, Yuhua Xu, Jie Chen et al.
- **Year**: 2021
- **Journal**: Journal of Alzheimer's Disease, 83(3)
- **DOI**: 10.3233/JAD-210141
- **Data**: NHANES 2011-2014, ≥60 years (n=2,564)

## Key Findings
- PNI = 10 × serum albumin (g/dL) + 0.005 × lymphocyte count
- Lower quartile of 4 cognitive tests → cognitive function impairment (CFI)
- Normal PNI → significantly lower odds of CFI vs low PNI
- Normal PNI participants had lower ratio of poor cognitive performance on global cognitive scores
- Low PNI group had reduced intake of protein, dietary fiber, saturated fatty acids, multiple micronutrients
- PNI is a good predictor of CFI in elderly

## Relevance to Our Study
**Critical paper** — The only PNI+cognition study in NHANES 2011-2014 we know of. Uses same dataset. Establishes PNI as predictor of cognitive impairment. Nutrient intake analysis adds depth. Did NOT examine DII, CONUT, GNRI, or depression mediation alongside PNI. GAP: no head-to-head comparison.
