# Association Between the Geriatric Nutritional Risk Index and Cognitive Functions in Older Adults

- **Authors**: Zhichun Tan, Yaxin Nie, Ning Yan
- **Year**: 2024
- **Journal**: Frontiers in Nutrition, 11:1459638
- **DOI**: 10.3389/fnut.2024.1459638
- **Data**: NHANES 2011-2014, ≥60 years (n=2,592)

## Key Findings
- GNRI = 1.489 × albumin (g/L) + 41.7 × (weight/ideal weight)
- GNRI categorized as low (<98) and high (≥98)
- Higher GNRI associated with better cognitive function on CERAD, AF, DSST, composite Z-score
- Smoothed curves and threshold effect analyses conducted
- But: within normal nutritional status, higher GNRI may reach a ceiling or even reverse
- GNRI could serve as tool for predicting cognitive function changes in clinical setting

## Relevance to Our Study
GNRI+cognition in same NHANES 2011-2014. Confirms malnutrition-cognition link. Unique: suggests possible ceiling effect within normal range. No comparison with other indices or depression mediation.
