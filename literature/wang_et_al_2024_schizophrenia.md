# Correlations Between Nutritional Indicators and Cognitive Function in Patients with Stable Schizophrenia

- **Authors**: Binyou Wang, Yong Zhou, Han Yu, Techeng Jiang, Kezhi Liu, Jianlin Pu et al.
- **Year**: 2024
- **Journal**: PLoS ONE, 19(11):e0312227
- **DOI**: 10.1371/journal.pone.0312227
- **Data**: Hospitalized schizophrenia patients, China (n=235)

## Key Findings
- **Only published study directly comparing PNI, GNRI, and CONUT for cognitive function**
- Only PNI was significantly associated with MoCA-C scores (r=0.15, p=0.021)
- GNRI and CONUT: not significantly associated with cognition
- PNI: β=0.147 (95%CI 0.049-0.351, p=0.01) in adjusted linear regression
- 90.2% of patients <60 years old — younger population

## Relevance to Our Study
**Only head-to-head comparison of PNI/GNRI/CONUT for cognition in ANY population.** Found PNI superior. But: schizophrenia population, younger, different cognitive test (MoCA-C vs NHANES CERAD/AF/DSST). Cannot directly generalize to community-dwelling elderly. **GAP: no head-to-head comparison in general elderly NHANES population.**
